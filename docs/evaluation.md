# Coverage, readability and reproducibility

## Grading a generated proof

The project tested two Lean solutions to the same course-style exercise. Let $s,t$ be finite sets, suppose $f$ maps $s$ into $t$, and give each $x\in s$ a natural weight $w(x)$. If $|t|b<\sum_{x\in s}w(x)$, prove **by comparison of finite sums** that some $y\in t$ has $\sum_{x\in s,\ f(x)=y}w(x)>b$. The two submissions have identical hypotheses and conclusions. [Original Lean submissions](../tests/fixtures/FiberSubmissions.lean)

The project fixed four criteria, worth one point each, before grading the papers. An imported identity or general comparison lemma can establish a step when the paper shows how its premises are met.

1. Regroup the total weight as a sum over fibers indexed by $t$, using the maps-into hypothesis.
2. Rewrite $|t|b$ as a constant sum and exhibit a strict comparison of two sums over $t$.
3. Infer that some fiber has weight greater than $b$ from that strict comparison.
4. Obtain a witness in $t$ and the requested bound for that witness.

Independent AI readers graded the generated papers using this rubric. They awarded **A 1/4**. A invokes the weighted pigeonhole theorem with both hypotheses, which establishes the conclusion and witness. They awarded **B 4/4**. B evaluates the constant sum, applies the strict hypothesis, groups the weights by $f$ using the maps-into condition, and applies a general comparison lemma to obtain a larger summand. Lean accepted both answers. A professor has yet to grade these papers or evaluate the project rubric. [Paper A](../papers/gradeability-fiber-a.pdf) · [A's LaTeX](../papers/gradeability-fiber-a.tex) · [Paper B](../papers/gradeability-fiber-b.pdf) · [B's LaTeX](../papers/gradeability-fiber-b.tex)

Both graders of the first editions had to connect the symbol for repeated addition to ordinary multiplication. The revised papers spell out the abstract sum as $\sum_{k=1}^{|t|} b$. B explicitly gives $\sum_{y\in t} b=|t|b$ for natural weights. A third PDF-only grader scored the revised B and A in that order and awarded 4/4 and 1/4. In A the grader still had to relate the abstract prerequisite to the product in the statement. Custom scalar actions keep their own notation. [Scalar-action test cases](../tests/fixtures/RepeatedAddition.lean)

## What the reader tests found

AI readers worked from generated PDFs. Their reports identify particular arguments they could reconstruct and places where the prose or notation obscured the reasoning.

- On the previously unseen dissociated-sets file, a reader recovered why the empty set is dissociated in Lemma 6 and how equal products supply a spanning expression in Theorem 28. The final proof opens by choosing the empty set. That witness establishes that the family of dissociated subsets is nonempty. The spanning witness is the maximal member chosen later on page 46. The reader had to reconstruct the purpose of the first witness. [Compile the original input](configuration.md#compile-a-file-in-another-lake-project)

- In the two-page Bell article a reader identified the last binomial factor in the product, the positivity needed for division, and where the proof uses the nonzero block-size assumption. The reader originally had to infer the final factor. The compiler now prints its index and value. In the three-page matching article another reader identified the edge-count witness and the imported degree-sum formula. Each short paper selects two declarations from the original Lean file. [Bell recurrence and factorial quotient](../papers/uniform-bell.pdf) · [Parity of matchings](../papers/matching-parity.pdf)

- In the first complete Dyck-word edition an induction proving an inverse law was easy to mistake for the main node-count induction. Proving recurring arguments as auxiliary lemmas reduced the article from 545 pages to 230. A fresh PDF-only grader located both maps on page 2, distinguished the two inverse identities from the main induction on pages 227–229, and identified why both recursive calls use smaller words. The grader inferred the extra root from the displayed sum of subtree sizes plus one. [Dyck words and binary trees](../papers/mathlib-dyck-words.pdf)
- In the Catalan paper, a reader initially had to reconstruct the telescoping function from the middle of the closed-form proof. Its checked defining equation D6 now appears on page 1. The reader verified which subtraction is truncated, the sign of the other factor, and the value at zero. The reader could also grade the binary-tree recurrence, including why the child-size classes are disjoint. A remaining difficulty in Lemma 5 is that the forward difference on pages 21–22 and its endpoint evaluation on page 38 are sixteen pages apart. [Catalan numbers and binary trees](../papers/mathlib-catalan.pdf)
- In the integer-partition paper, a reader explained how equality of the parts multisets identifies two partitions of the same integer. Once the multisets agree, the remaining fields prove the same positivity and sum conditions. The reader identified the later use on page 10, where equality of the count of every part implies multiset equality and hence equality of partitions. The converse implication on page 4 took extra work to read. [Integer partitions](../papers/mathlib-integer-partitions.pdf)
- In an earlier full graph-matching edition, a reader identified the edge-count witness, the degree-one and degree-sum steps, and the spanning assumption that extends the parity result to the whole graph. The reader had to supply the intermediate sum of ones. Three repetitions of the same equality made it harder to find the step that doubled the edge count. [Matchings in graphs](../papers/mathlib-matching.pdf)

- In the Hamiltonian paper, a reader recovered the singleton-graph convention, both length formulas, the start vertex's two occurrences in a cycle, and the walk used to connect any two distinct vertices. After an earlier correction the reader could follow the visit counts becoming a sum of ones. Lemma 7 now ends by subtracting one from both sides of the computed equality. The full-detail setting retains the eight order and cancellation steps behind that subtraction. Lemma 4 introduces its arbitrary vertex and proves the upper and lower bounds in separate paragraphs. [Hamiltonian paths and cycles](../papers/mathlib-hamiltonian.pdf)
- In the double-counting paper, a reader recovered the incidence sum and the totality and uniqueness assumptions behind a cardinality bound. In the powerset paper, a reader caught an ambiguous grouping of union and difference. [Double counting](../papers/mathlib-double-counting.pdf) · [Powersets](../papers/mathlib-powerset.pdf)
- Readers recovered both inductive cases of Hall's theorem and the five equalities in weighted inclusion-exclusion. Their reports led to introducing a chosen representative together with its membership and printing the bound on an indexed intersection. [Hall](../papers/mathlib-hall.pdf) · [Inclusion-exclusion](../papers/mathlib-inclusion-exclusion.pdf)
- A reader recovered the final inequality in Sperner's theorem, including the LYM bound and multiplication by a positive denominator. In the derangements paper a reader could reconstruct the recurrence and alternating sum but initially could not identify the bijection used to count the two cases. The revised paper states the imported bijection and both alternatives in its target. [Sperner](../papers/mathlib-lym.pdf) · [Derangements](../papers/mathlib-derangements.pdf)
- In the converse direction of Turán's theorem, a reader initially needed justification for two cardinality equalities. The reader found both in the revised edition before the graph isomorphisms were composed. The full paper in that reader trial ran to 172 pages. [Turán](../papers/mathlib-turan.pdf)

## What the compiler finished

A declaration counts as complete when the renderer explains or cites its proof throughout. The first thirteen complete Mathlib files contained **367 selected declarations**, including aliases. Three further files contribute 11 results on Bell numbers, 44 on Dyck words, and 14 on Catalan numbers, giving **436 selected declarations across sixteen files**. The first frozen runs completed 9/9 for derangements, 15/15 for LYM, 12/17 for Hamiltonian graphs and 35/46 for matchings. [Eleven-file resource benchmark](../benchmarks/mathlib-paper-coverage.tsv) · [Earlier trial](../benchmarks/mathlib-coverage.tsv) · [Articles and original sources](examples.md#a-paper-built-from-a-complete-library-file)

All 22 double-counting results now have different titles. One pair distinguishes a strict upper bound on column degrees from a strict lower bound on row degrees. Other articles still have repeated titles, including equivalent results given two names in the source. [Double counting](../papers/mathlib-double-counting.pdf)

In the graph-matching paper, Lemma 18 chooses the edge count as a witness for evenness. Each vertex of the matching has degree one, so the sum of degrees counts its vertices. The degree-sum theorem equates that sum to twice the edge count. Explaining this proof required recovering a checked equality between propositions. Later results also required extracting a witness with its set-membership proof and applying an implication contained in a conjunction. [Generated paper](../papers/mathlib-matching.pdf) · [Original source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/c44e0c8ee63ca166450922a373c7409c5d26b00b/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Matching.lean)

On the original Bell file, coverage rose from 7/11 to 11/11 after the compiler learned to recover the predecessor induction hypothesis. This file proves product and factorial identities. Mathlib leaves their interpretation as counts of set partitions as future work. On the Dyck-word file, coverage rose from 15/44 to 44/44 after checking equality of records, recursion on two subtrees, and the decreasing measure for both parts of a word. The compiler also renders the previously missing defining equation. On the Catalan file, coverage rose from 10/14 to 14/14 after recovering a checked polynomial identity from the telescoping proof. [Bell source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/c44e0c8ee63ca166450922a373c7409c5d26b00b/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/Bell.lean) · [Dyck-word source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/c44e0c8ee63ca166450922a373c7409c5d26b00b/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/DyckWord.lean) · [Catalan source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/c44e0c8ee63ca166450922a373c7409c5d26b00b/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/Catalan.lean)

The next frozen whole-file trial, on integer partitions, completed **9/13 proofs** and rendered all selected defining equations. The four incomplete proofs were the structure extensionality lemma and three results depending on it. Checking equality of the parts multisets also involves transporting the proof that their sum equals the partition's index. With that check, the same unmodified file reached **13/13**, bringing the combined total to **449 declarations in seventeen files**. An independently named record of fixed-width decimal digits exercises the same reasoning with lists. [Original partition source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/c44e0c8ee63ca166450922a373c7409c5d26b00b/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/Partition.lean) · [Independent Lean examples](../tests/fixtures/IndexedRecordTrial.lean)

After the seventeen-file evaluation, two additional files were selected by topic, size and availability before translation. With the compiler frozen, the ordinary command completed **28/28** declarations on dissociated sets and **20/21** on directed graphs. In the last directed-graph result, Lean establishes that a nonempty vertex type admits two distinct directed graphs. The compiler left a visible gap when translating a proposition proved equal to true. Both original files were used whole. These two results measure first-run coverage on new inputs. ## Compilation resources

The eleven-file table separates elaborating and exporting the original Lean source from rendering its checked export with C++. In the recorded Windows runs, the 131-declaration finite-set cardinality file took about **30 seconds** to check and export, followed by **4 seconds** to render, with about **1 GiB** peak memory in C++. The 19-declaration Turán file took about **41 seconds** to check and export and **13 seconds** to render, with about **3.6 GiB** peak memory in C++. The larger proof terms in the latter file make declaration count a poor timing estimate by itself. The timings are individual runs with prebuilt dependencies. [Per-file timings, memory and export sizes](../benchmarks/mathlib-paper-coverage.tsv)

The original four-file benchmark also timed standalone Lean checking and PDF typesetting. Its end-to-end total adds source elaboration and export, C++ rendering and typesetting. It sampled the process-tree working set every 20 ms on Windows with a Core Ultra 9 185H and 31.5 GiB of RAM. Downloading dependencies and building the compiler took place outside the timed stages. ## Reproducibility and earlier experiments

The largest measured gap between ordinary words in Theorem 1 and Lemma 4 of the Hamiltonian paper fell from 29.27 and 41.50 points, respectively, to 4.97 points in each proof. An independent check hit-tested every printed letter and digit of 118 citations in the proof text, verified the destination against the printed heading, and checked that adjacent words were outside the link. Across all seventeen complete Mathlib papers, the same check verified 9,296 citation phrases, including 42 citations to auxiliary lemmas. [Corrected PDF](../papers/mathlib-hamiltonian.pdf) · [Navigation test](../tests/verify_pdf_navigation.py)

An independent compilation reproduced the published LaTeX, page dimensions, extracted text, and rendered pixels at 72 dpi for five papers. These comprised the Hamiltonian paper, the two short Bell and matching selections, the full Catalan paper, and the integer-partition paper, totaling 82 pages. The full-source verification also compared each of the seventeen original Lean files with its Git object in the pinned Mathlib checkout.

The clean-build verifier compares generated LaTeX with the published papers. In the earlier arithmetic reproduction study, an independent typesetting also matched the text, page dimensions and rendered pixels at 144 dpi. The current compact and expanded arithmetic editions include the new paper layout. [Compact LaTeX](../papers/arithmetic-certificates.tex) · [Expanded LaTeX](../papers/arithmetic-certificates-expanded.tex) · [Reproduction commands](configuration.md#reproduce-the-examples)

The initial number-theory experiment used three development proofs and two reserved proofs. The frozen C++ templates narrated all five. A reader reconstructed both reserved arguments from the document alone. The curator had shared a high-level description of the methods before the freeze. A separate checked `omega` proof left a placeholder in that early translator. [Structured output](../examples/structured.tex) · [Initial automation output](../examples/automation.tex)

In the chapter pilot, the prototype finished 4/10 previously selected divisibility proofs. The extended compiler finished all ten original proofs. After a second freeze it rendered all five proofs in the progression section on the first run. Readers of the chapter alone reconstructed the decisive steps in Theorems 10, 16 and 23.
