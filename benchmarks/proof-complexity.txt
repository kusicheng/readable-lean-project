A theorem score includes its tactic. In this example the extra four points
are the bindings for two variables and two hypotheses. The paper totals sum
the theorem scores once each. Only individual proofs and supported arithmetic
calls use the expansion budget. Definitions, prerequisites, and page count
are outside these totals. A cited theorem contributes an application at its
point of use. Its original proof contributes to its own theorem score.

The first three rows contribute 175 of 218 points (80.3%). Seven applications
of rules in the Linarith namespace cost 21 points. Setting only the certificate
weight to zero would still give this call 197 points. The compact proof takes
three sentences to give the contradiction. The expanded proof takes seven.

The default budget is 24. A score above the budget selects compact narration.
At the exact budget the renderer uses expanded narration. These weights are
heuristics for presentation. They have no human-reading-time calibration.

How the counts were collected

Lean 4.19.0 ran tests/fixtures/Arithmetic.lean against Mathlib v4.19.0.
The per-name rows were collected by logging each increment in a local build
of src/proof_cost.cpp. Every category count and total was checked against the
ordinary compiler diagnostics. Each occurrence in a proof argument counts.
Reachable generated helper bodies are traversed once per scored proof.
The score excludes elapsed time and unsuccessful tactic search.

Compiler paths

  src/proof_cost.hpp       Default weights
  src/proof_cost.cpp       Traversal, operation classification and weighted sum
  src/tactic_proof.cpp     Budget decision on the actual tactic assignment
  src/narration.cpp        Enclosing theorem budget decision
  papers/arithmetic-certificates.tex           Compact output
  papers/arithmetic-certificates-expanded.tex  Expanded output
